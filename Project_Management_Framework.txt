================================================================================
                    PROJECT MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORKS GUIDE
================================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION TO PROJECT MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A project management framework is a structured set of guidelines, processes, 
and best practices designed to plan, execute, manage, and deliver projects 
efficiently. 

Choosing the right framework depends on team size, project complexity, 
industry requirements, stakeholder involvement, and predictability.


2. TRADITIONAL & SEQUENTIAL FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ideal for projects with well-defined requirements, predictable outcomes, and 
strict regulatory or compliance demands.

A. WATERFALL
   - Overview: A linear, phase-by-phase approach where each phase (Requirements, 
     Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, Maintenance) must be completed 
     before the next begins.
   - Best For: Construction, hardware manufacturing, enterprise software with 
     fixed requirements.
   - Pros: Clear documentation, predictable milestones, easy to understand.
   - Cons: Inflexible; difficult and expensive to accommodate late changes.

B. PRINCE2 (Projects IN Controlled Environments)
   - Overview: A process-based framework widely used in the UK and internationally. 
     Focuses on organization, management, and control throughout the project lifecycle.
   - Key Principles: Continued business justification, defined roles/responsibilities, 
     management by stages, management by exception.
   - Best For: Government projects, large enterprise initiatives.
   - Pros: Standardized, clear accountability, business-case driven.
   - Cons: Can require heavy documentation and administrative overhead.

C. CPM (CRITICAL PATH METHOD)
   - Overview: A step-by-step technique for process planning that defines critical 
     and non-critical tasks with the goal of preventing timelines and bottlenecks.
   - Best For: Complex multi-dependent projects like engineering and infrastructure.


3. AGILE & ITERATIVE FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ideal for fast-paced, high-uncertainty environments where flexible response to 
change is critical.

A. SCRUM
   - Overview: An iterative framework based on short work cycles called "Sprints" 
     (typically 1 to 4 weeks long).
   - Core Roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team.
   - Key Events: Sprint Planning, Daily Standup, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.
   - Best For: Software development, product design, creative agencies.
   - Pros: Fast delivery cycles, continuous feedback, highly adaptable.
   - Cons: Scope creep if not well-managed; requires experienced team discipline.

B. KANBAN
   - Overview: A visual workflow management method focused on continuous delivery 
     and limiting Work-in-Progress (WIP).
   - Core Tool: Kanban Board (Columns like To-Do, In Progress, Review, Done).
   - Best For: Operations, maintenance teams, continuous product updates.
   - Pros: Flexible priority adjustments, visual transparency, steady workflow.
   - Cons: Less structured for time-bound milestones without explicit sprints.

C. LEAN DEVELOPMENT
   - Overview: Adapted from Lean Manufacturing, focusing on maximizing customer value 
     while eliminating waste (delays, unnecessary features, excessive motion).
   - Best For: Startups, resource-constrained teams seeking high efficiency.

D. EXTREME PROGRAMMING (XP)
   - Overview: An Agile sub-framework emphasized in software engineering that stresses 
     technical quality, pair programming, automated testing, and frequent releases.


4. HYBRID & PROCESS IMPROVEMENT FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Combines elements from multiple frameworks to handle complex, evolving projects.

A. HYBRID AGILE-WATERFALL
   - Overview: Uses Waterfall for upfront planning, design, and architecture, while 
     employing Scrum or Kanban for iterative development and testing.
   - Best For: Hybrid projects blending hardware and software development.

B. SIX SIGMA / DMAIC
   - Overview: A data-driven framework focused on defect reduction and quality control.
   - DMAIC Phases: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control.
   - Best For: Quality assurance, manufacturing optimization, process improvement.


5. STRATEGIC & GOAL-SETTING FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. PMBOK PROCESS GROUPS (PMI)
   - Overview: Maintained by the Project Management Institute (PMI), structuring 
     projects into 5 Process Groups:
     1. Initiating
     2. Planning
     3. Executing
     4. Monitoring & Controlling
     5. Closing
   - Applied alongside 10 Knowledge Areas (Scope, Schedule, Cost, Quality, Risk, etc.).

B. OKRs (OBJECTIVES AND KEY RESULTS)
   - Overview: A goal-setting framework used to align project outcomes with overall 
     organizational vision.
   - Objective: High-level qualitative goal.
   - Key Results: 3-5 quantitative metrics defining success.


6. FRAMEWORK SELECTION CHECKSHEET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use the following criteria to choose the right framework:

Criteria               | Recommended Framework
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixed scope & budget   | Waterfall or PRINCE2
Fast-changing market   | Scrum or Kanban
Continuous workflow    | Kanban
Quality/Defect focus   | Six Sigma
Large-scale hybrid    | Hybrid (Waterfall + Agile)
Startup / MVP focus    | Lean / Scrum
