                     IT SERVICE DESK & CUSTOMER HANDLING SOP

Document Name : Customer_handle_in_IT.txt
Target Audience: Helpdesk Technicians, IT Support Engineers, Service Desk Leads
Objective    : Standard Operating Procedures & Best Practices for IT Support

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CORE COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Active Listening: Let the user finish explaining their issue before responding.
  Never interrupt, even if you know the answer immediately.
- Translate Technical Jargon: Speak in clear, non-technical terms. 
  Example: Instead of "Clear your DNS cache and renew your DHCP lease," 
  say "We need to reset your computer's network connection."
- Empathy First: Acknowledge the user's frustration before technical troubleshooting.
  Example: "I understand how crucial this presentation is, and I'll help you get 
  access to this folder right away."
- Ownership & Accountability: Take responsibility for the ticket. Avoid using 
  phrases like "That's not my job" or "The network team messed up."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. STEP-BY-STEP TICKET & CALL HANDLING PROCESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Greeting & Identification
  - Greet the user professionally.
  - Verify identity (Name, Employee ID, Department, Contact Number).
  - Record the machine name/asset ID.

Step 2: Issue Discovery & Categorization
  - Ask open-ended questions: "What error message are you seeing?"
  - Ask targeted questions: "When did this issue start?" / "Is anyone else affected?"
  - Categorize priority: Critical (P1), High (P2), Medium (P3), Low (P4).

Step 3: Troubleshooting & Communication
  - Explain what steps you are taking before performing actions (especially when 
    using Remote Desktop software like TeamViewer, AnyDesk, or Bomgar).
  - Keep the user engaged if a scan or install takes time.

Step 4: Verification & Resolution
  - Have the user test the solution themselves before closing the call/ticket.
  - Ask: "Is everything working as expected now?"

Step 5: Documentation & Closure
  - Log clear summary notes in the ITSM tool (ServiceNow, Jira, Zendesk, etc.):
    * Symptom reported
    * Root cause
    * Resolution steps taken
  - Send a formal ticket closure notification.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MANAGING IRATE OR DIFFICULT CUSTOMERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Technique: The HEAT Model
  - H (Hear): Allow the customer to vent without interruption.
  - E (Empathize): Validate their emotional state. ("I completely understand why 
    this delay is frustrating.")
  - A (Acknowledge): Reiterate the issue to show comprehension. ("So to confirm, 
    you cannot log into the financial portal before the 5 PM deadline.")
  - T (Take Action): Outline the immediate action plan with clear deadlines.

De-escalation Rules:
  - Stay calm and maintain a neutral, steady voice tone.
  - Do not take attacks personally—the user is frustrated at the system, not you.
  - Never argue or prove the user wrong in an aggressive manner.
  - If a user becomes verbally abusive, follow protocol:
    1. Issue a calm warning: "I want to help you, but I need us to keep this conversation professional."
    2. If abuse continues, escalate to your direct supervisor according to company policy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. EMAIL & TICKET WRITING BEST PRACTICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Always include Ticket ID in the subject line (e.g., [INC-104928]).
- Keep instructions numbered and easy to follow.
- Use screenshots with highlights/arrows whenever possible.
- Set clear SLAs (Service Level Agreements) for updates.
  Bad: "We will fix it soon."
  Good: "Our L2 team is investigating. I will update you by 2:00 PM EST."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. QUICK DO'S AND DON'TS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DO:
  [+] Set realistic expectations—under-promise and over-deliver.
  [+] Keep the customer updated regularly on long-running incidents.
  [+] Double-check ticket notes for typos before submitting.

DON'T:
  [-] Don't use negative phrasing ("You can't do that" -> "Here is how we can do that").
  [-] Don't leave a remote session without informing the user.
  [-] Don't assume the user's technical level—adapt to each individual.
