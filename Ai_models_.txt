                       AI MODELS OVERVIEW
===================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION TO AI MODELS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
An AI model is a mathematical or algorithmic framework trained on
data to perform specific tasks such as prediction, classification, 
generation, or decision-making without being explicitly programmed 
for every rule.

2. MAJOR ARCHITECTURES & MODEL TYPES
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Large Language Models (LLMs) & Transformers
   - Architecture: Self-Attention Mechanism (Transformer architecture)
   - Function: Process sequential text data, understand context, 
     and generate human-like language.
   - Examples: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, LLaMA, Mistral.
   - Common Uses: Text generation, translation, coding assistance, 
     summarization.

B. Vision Models & Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)
   - Architecture: Grid-based feature extraction (Convolutions).
   - Function: Recognize patterns, textures, and spatial features 
     in images and videos.
   - Examples: ResNet, EfficientNet, YOLO (You Only Look Once).
   - Common Uses: Image classification, object detection, medical 
     imaging, facial recognition.

C. Diffusion & Generative Vision Models
   - Architecture: Iterative denoising process / Latent Diffusion.
   - Function: Synthesize high-resolution imagery and video from text 
     prompts or low-dimensional noise.
   - Examples: Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Flux, Sora.
   - Common Uses: Visual art creation, asset generation, design.

D. Multimodal Models
   - Architecture: Vision-Language Transformers (VLTs), unified 
     cross-attention models.
   - Function: Process and connect multiple data modalities simultaneously 
     (e.g., text, vision, audio, code).
   - Examples: Gemini Pro/Ultra, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
   - Common Uses: Document understanding, visual Q&A, audio-driven UI.

E. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) & LSTMs
   - Architecture: Sequential processing with memory gates.
   - Function: Handle time-series or sequential data where history 
     in sequence matters.
   - Examples: Standard LSTM networks, GRU architectures.
   - Common Uses: Time-series forecasting, speech recognition, financial 
     market prediction.

F. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Models
   - Architecture: Agent-environment interaction using reward functions.
   - Function: Learn optimal actions through trial and error to maximize 
     long-term rewards.
   - Examples: Deep Q-Networks (DQN), PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), 
     AlphaZero.
   - Common Uses: Game AI, robotics control, autonomous driving, system 
     optimization.

3. TRAINING PARADIGMS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Supervised Learning: Learning from labeled dataset pairs (Input -> Label).
- Unsupervised Learning: Discovering patterns/clusters in unlabeled data.
- Self-Supervised Learning: Generating targets directly from raw data (e.g., 
  next-token prediction).
- Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF): Aligning generated 
  outputs with human preferences and safety protocols.

4. KEY EVALUATION METRICS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Text / Generation: Perplexity, BLEU score, ROUGE score, Human ELO ratings.
- Classification / Vision: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, mAP.
- Regression: Mean Squared Error (MSE), R-squared (R²).
