===================================================================
                    GOOGLE ANTIGRAVITY / GOOGLE GRAVITY
===================================================================

1. OVERVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Google Antigravity (often referenced interchangeably with Google Gravity) 
is an interactive web-based Easter egg and physics experiment originally 
created by Ricardo Cabello (Mr.doob) in 2009 using HTML5, JavaScript, 
and 2D physics engines.

Instead of a static Google search homepage, the interface components 
(the Google logo, search bar, buttons, and navigation links) float 
upward or collapse under simulated gravity.


2. HOW IT WORKS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Physics Engine: Uses JavaScript physics libraries (such as Box2DJS 
  or Matter.js) to treat DOM elements as dynamic rigid bodies.
- Drag & Throw: Users can click and drag individual page elements 
  (like the "I'm Feeling Lucky" button or Google logo) and toss them 
  around the browser viewport.
- Fully Functional Search: Although the buttons and search bar are 
  scrambled or floating, typing into the search bar and hitting Enter 
  will still fetch search results, which then fall/float into the window.


3. KEY VARIATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
* Google Gravity:
  The elements fall down to the bottom of the screen as if pulled 
  by normal Earth gravity.

* Google Antigravity (Zero Gravity):
  The elements float slowly upward toward the top or drift freely 
  across the screen as if floating in outer space / zero-G.


4. HOW TO TRY IT
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Open any web browser and go to elgoog.im/gravity or elgoog.im/underwater
2. Alternatively, search "Google Gravity" or "Google Anti Gravity" on 
   Google and click "I'm Feeling Lucky".
3. Move your mouse or tap elements to interact with the physics.


5. HISTORICAL CONTEXT
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Created during the late 2000s era of web experiments to demonstrate the 
growing power of modern browsers executing complex JavaScript physics 
and HTML5 canvas animations without needing third-party plugins like Flash.
