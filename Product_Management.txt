                       PRODUCT MANAGEMENT: AN END-TO-END GUIDE
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS PRODUCT MANAGEMENT?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Product Management (PM) sits at the intersection of business, technology, and
user experience (UX). A Product Manager is responsible for guiding the success
of a product and leading the cross-functional team that delivers it.

Core Objective: Deliver value to users while solving key business problems.

Key Responsibilities:
- Strategy & Vision: Defining where the product is going and why.
- Execution: Working with engineering, design, and QA to build features.
- Analysis: Measuring performance and making data-informed decisions.
- Communication: Aligning stakeholders and cross-functional teams.


2. THE PRODUCT LIFE CYCLE (PLC)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Introduction: Market research, building the MVP, launch strategy.
2. Growth: Scaling user acquisition, optimizing onboarding, rapid feature iteration.
3. Maturity: Retention focus, market expansion, optimization, monetization.
4. Decline: Sunsetting, pivoting, or cost reduction.


3. KEY PM CONCEPTS & TERMINOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- MVP (Minimum Viable Product): The simplest version of a product that allows a 
  team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers.
- PRD (Product Requirement Document): Defines the product's purpose, features, 
  functionality, and success metrics.
- Roadmap: High-level visual summary that maps out the direction of your product 
  over time.
- Backlog: Prioritized list of tasks, features, and bug fixes to be worked on.
- Product-Market Fit (PMF): The degree to which a product satisfies a strong 
  market demand.


4. FEATURE PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. RICE Framework:
   Score = (Reach * Impact * Confidence) / Effort
   - Reach: How many people will this impact in a given timeframe?
   - Impact: How much will this increase satisfaction/conversion? (0.25 to 3)
   - Confidence: Percentage confidence in estimates (e.g., 80%).
   - Effort: Person-months required.

B. MoSCoW Method:
   - Must Have: Non-negotiable requirements for launch.
   - Should Have: Important, but not strictly required for v1.
   - Could Have: Nice-to-haves if time and budget permit.
   - Won't Have: Out of scope for current cycle.

C. Kano Model:
   Categorizes features based on customer perception:
   - Basic Needs (Expected features)
   - Performance Needs (Linear satisfaction)
   - Delighters (Unexpected features that excite users)


5. ESSENTIAL PM METRICS & KPIs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Acquisition: CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost), Organic vs. Paid Traffic.
- Engagement: DAU/MAU (Daily/Monthly Active Users), Session Duration.
- Retention: Churn Rate, N-Day Retention, Retention Curves.
- Revenue: LTV (Lifetime Value), MRR/ARR (Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue).
- Satisfaction: NPS (Net Promoter Score), CSAT (Customer Satisfaction).


6. STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT & COMMUNICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Align early and often with Engineering, UX/UI, Sales, Marketing, and Leadership.
- Focus on "Why" and "What" before discussing "How".
- Use data to back up product decisions and manage conflicting priorities.
