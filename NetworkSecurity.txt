                     NETWORK SECURITY OVERVIEW
======================================================================

1. WHAT IS NETWORK SECURITY?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Network security is a broad term that covers a set of rules, 
technologies, and processes designed to protect the integrity, 
confidentiality, and accessibility of computer networks and data. 
It includes both hardware and software technologies.

The ultimate goal is to prevent unauthorized access, misuse, 
modification, or denial of a computer network and network-accessible 
resources.


2. THE THREE PILLARS (CIA TRIAD)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Effective network security relies on three core concepts:

* Confidentiality: Ensuring that data is accessible only to those 
  authorized to have access.
* Integrity: Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of data, 
  ensuring it cannot be modified in transit by unauthorized parties.
* Availability: Ensuring that authorized users have reliable and 
  timely access to data and resources when needed.


3. COMMON TYPES OF NETWORK SECURITY TOOLS & STRATEGIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
To secure a network, organizations deploy a multi-layered defense system:

* Firewalls: Devices or software that monitor and control incoming 
  and outgoing network traffic based on predetermined security rules. 
  They act as a barrier between trusted and untrusted networks.

* Access Control: Defining which users and devices have access to 
  network applications and systems. This often utilizes Network 
  Access Control (NAC) to check devices before granting entry.

* Antivirus and Antimalware Software: Tools designed to detect, 
  prevent, and remove malicious software (like viruses, ransomware, 
  worms, and spyware) from the network.

* Virtual Private Networks (VPNs): Services that encrypt the 
  connection from a device to a network over the internet, ensuring 
  that data transmission remains private and secure.

* Network Segmentation: The practice of splitting a network into 
  smaller, distinct subnetworks. If a breach occurs in one zone, 
  the damage is contained and cannot easily spread to others.

* Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS): Systems that actively scan 
  network traffic to detect and block attacks in real-time.


4. PRIMARY NETWORK THREATS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Security measures are built to defend against various digital threats, 
including:

* Malware: Malicious software designed to damage or exploit devices.
* Phishing: Deceptive emails or messages aimed at tricking users 
  into revealing sensitive information like passwords.
* Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attacks: Where an attacker intercepts 
  and alters communications between two parties without their knowledge.
* Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS): An attempt to crash a 
  server or network by overwhelming it with a flood of internet traffic.


5. BEST PRACTICES FOR EVERYDAY SECURITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
* Use Strong Passwords & Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
* Keep all software, operating systems, and firmware updated (patching).
* Train employees and users regularly to recognize social engineering 
  and phishing attempts.
* Back up critical data frequently and store backups securely off-network.
